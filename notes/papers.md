# Jack Morava: selected writings and reading trail

**Working status:** 2026-10-09. This is an annotated *bibliography and abstract-level reading plan*, not a proof-by-proof treatment. The descriptions are paraphrases of the linked publication abstracts and nLab index. Distinguish statements made by a paper from conjectures, questions, and the proposed visualizations.

## Origins: chromatic topology and cobordism

- **Jack Morava, “Bording School” seminar notes**, Princeton, approximately 1973, scan preserved by **Haynes Miller**. Early material leading toward chromatic homotopy. The [nLab author page](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Jack+Morava) links scans. **Reading question:** what did the original viewpoint make clear before today's spectral language? **Visual hook:** prime and height are independent coordinates in a family of invariants.
- **Jack Morava, “Cobordism of symplectic manifolds and asymptotic expansions”** (1999), [arXiv:math/9908070](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9908070), *Proceedings of the Steklov Institute of Mathematics* 225. Identifies a symplectic cobordism ring introduced by **Victor Ginzburg** with a Pontrjagin ring of complex-oriented manifolds carrying free circle actions, and points toward an interpretation of the formal group law through compositions of asymptotic expansions. **Visual hook:** circle action and gluing, with orientation and boundary conditions shown explicitly.
- **Jack Morava, “Toward a fundamental groupoid for the stable homotopy category”** (2005; published later), [arXiv:math/0509001](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0509001). *Explicitly speculative*: uses analogies with nearby cycles and families of ring spectra. **Visual hook:** an incidence diagram of strata and their specializations, not a literal picture of a “fundamental groupoid of spectra.”
- **Jack Morava, “Complex cobordism and algebraic topology”** (2007), [arXiv:0707.3216](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.3216). Historical survey. **Reading question:** identify the path from complex orientations and formal group laws to chromatic decomposition.

## Configuration spaces, braid groups, and Galois analogies

- **Jack Morava, “The Cosmic Galois group as Koszul dual to Waldhausen's A(pt)”** (2011), [arXiv:1108.4627](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.4627). Proposes connections between Waldhausen K-theory, descent, Hopf algebras, quasi-symmetric functions, and mixed Tate motives. **Visual hook:** ordered compositions and quasi-symmetric functions; do not equate these combinatorial pictures with the speculative motivic group.
- **Jack Morava and Jonathan Beardsley, “Toward a Galois theory of the integers over the sphere spectrum”** (2018), [arXiv:1710.05992](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05992), *Journal of Geometry and Physics* 131, 41–51. Discusses Thom-spectrum models for ordinary integral homology, configuration spaces, monoidal functors, and derived Galois analogies. The paper describes itself as largely expository, with speculative directions. **Visual hook:** move particles in the plane, then show unordered configurations, braid paths, and group completion as separate constructions.
- **Jack Morava and Dale Rolfsen, “Toward the group completion of the Burau representation”** (2023), [arXiv:1809.01994](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01994), *Transactions of the AMS* 376, 1845–1865. Interprets the Burau braid representation using a monoidal topological groupoid of finite point sets; discusses writhe, group completion, Thom spectra, and a TQFT. **Visual hook:** three-strand braid on one side and exact Laurent-polynomial matrices on the other. See [the example](burau-braids.md).

## Symplectic and orbifold geometry

- **Alejandro Adem, Jack Morava, Yongbin Ruan (editors), *Orbifolds in Mathematics and Physics*** (2002), [AMS Contemporary Mathematics 310](https://doi.org/10.1090/conm/310). This is an *edited proceedings volume*, not a three-author monograph. The collection includes work by **Weimin Chen and Yongbin Ruan**, **Ieke Moerdijk**, **Ernesto Lupercio and Bernardo Uribe**, **Eric Sharpe**, and many other named contributors. **Visual hook:** quotient spaces paired with the stabilizer groups that a naive geometric quotient forgets.
- **Nitu Kitchloo and Jack Morava, “The stable symplectic category and a conjecture of Kontsevich”** (2018; circulated earlier as *The Grothendieck–Teichmüller group and the stable symplectic category*), [arXiv:1212.6905](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.6905), [DOI:10.1090/CONM/708/14269](https://doi.org/10.1090/CONM/708/14269). Studies an oriented stable symplectic category and a quotient of Grothendieck–Teichmüller acting by monoidal automorphisms. **Visual hook:** draw honest finite-dimensional Lagrangian correspondences first; the stabilization and motivic group must remain labeled as additional structure.
- **Jack Morava, “On formal groups and geometric quantization”** (2019), [arXiv:1905.06181](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06181). Connects complex projective spaces as prequantized symplectic objects with symmetric-function perspectives. **Visual hook:** projective spaces and their characteristic-number bookkeeping, without pretending to directly see a formal group law.

## Symmetry, strata, and unusual applications

- **Jack Morava, “On the canonical formula of C. Lévi-Strauss”** (2003), [arXiv:math/0306174](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0306174). Proposes an interpretation of a controversial formula for myth structures using an *anti*-automorphism of the quaternion group $Q_8$. **Visual hook:** the eight elements and the order-reversal identity $(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}$. The mathematical group does not settle a dispute in anthropology.
- **Jack Morava, “Theories of anything”** (2012), [arXiv:1202.0684](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.0684). Proposes a more structured analogue of $\pi_0$ for topological groupoids that preserves incidence relations among quotient strata; discusses Thom, **David Spivak** and categorical databases, and phase transitions. **Visual hook:** a geometrical orbit space beside the groupoid/isotropy data that it loses.
- **Jack Morava and Rekha Santhanam, “Power operations and Absolute geometry”** (2012), [author manuscript, nLab-hosted PDF](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/files/AbsolutePower.pdf). A conjectural comparison between homotopy power operations and absolute geometry. Their own abstract says the note is speculative. **Visual hook:** symmetric powers and operations on small finite sets, with any extrapolation to absolute geometry labeled conjectural.
- **Jack Morava, “Notes on δ-algebras and prisms in homotopy theory”** (2024), [arXiv:2401.12336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12336). Relates Frobenius lifts and power operations to prism structures and suggests extensions toward chromatic neighborhoods of K(n). **Visual hook:** finite truncated Witt-vector/p-adic arithmetic; do not identify Euclidean fractal textures with prisms.
- **Jack Morava, “Circular symmetry-breaking and topological Noether currents”** (2024), [arXiv:2407.00672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00672). Proposes a toy model involving bubbling, circle actions, Noether symmetry, and a collapse of ([S^2/mathbb T]). **Visual hook:** rotating sphere, latitude orbits, two fixed poles, quotient interval, and a separately explained collapse map. This is an ideal concrete geometric model.
- **Jack Morava, “Some very low-dimensional algebraic topology”** (2024), [arXiv:2411.15885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15885). Connects renormalization, a neighborhood of a Jordan curve near infinity on the Riemann sphere, and fields valued in (Omega S^2). **Visual hook:** spherical loops and compactification, while keeping QFT claims separate.
- **Jack Morava, “Boundary framings for locally conformally symplectic four-manifolds”** (2025), [arXiv:2502.05983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05983). Discusses classifying spaces, 4-manifolds, and a cobordism category with anchored 3-manifolds. **Visual hook:** local boundary framings and 3D slices of a 4D family; a 3D view is only a slice.
